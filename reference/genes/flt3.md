# FLT3-ITD and FLT3-TKD

> Reference monograph. Evidence trail closed **9 September 2026**. Where a claim rests on a conference presentation or press release rather than a peer-reviewed publication, this is stated. Evidence tier is labelled throughout: *approved*, *phase 3 positive*, *phase 3 negative*, *phase 1–2 signal*, *registry/retrospective*, *preclinical*.

## Biology

FLT3 (CD135) is a class III receptor tyrosine kinase, kin to KIT, CSF1R and PDGFRA/B: five extracellular immunoglobulin-like domains, a transmembrane segment, an autoinhibitory juxtamembrane domain (JMD), and a kinase domain split by a kinase insert into TKD1 and TKD2. FLT3 ligand (FLT3LG) drives dimerisation and autophosphorylation, activating STAT5, RAS/MAPK and PI3K/AKT; physiologically this governs haematopoietic progenitor and dendritic-cell development [1][3].

**ITD.** The duplication is always in frame and inserts into the JMD or, in a substantial minority, extends into the β1-sheet of TKD1. It abolishes JMD autoinhibition, producing ligand-independent constitutive kinase activity with a STAT5-biased signalling output that differs qualitatively from wild-type FLT3. Insertion length is highly variable; validated MRD assays must cover 3 bp to >200 bp, with ITDs up to 228 bp reported [16].

**TKD (D835/I836).** Activation-loop point substitutions (D835Y/V/H/E/N/F/I, I836 insertions/deletions). Their importance is conformational: D835 mutations stabilise the active "DFG-in" state. **Type II inhibitors** (quizartinib, sorafenib, ponatinib) require the inactive "DFG-out" conformation and are therefore intrinsically compromised against most D835 substitutions — bulky hydrophobic ones (D835Y/V/I/F) are most resistant, while those preserving the D835–S838 interaction (D835E/N) remain relatively sensitive. **Type I inhibitors** (gilteritinib, crenolanib) bind the active conformation and retain activity across D835 variants [3][20]. This one structural fact explains the divergence in labelling: quizartinib is licensed for ITD only, gilteritinib for ITD and D835/I836.

## Epidemiology and co-mutation landscape

Frequency must be read against its cohort. PETHEMA registry, **2901 adults given upfront intensive chemotherapy in the pre-FLT3-inhibitor era: 579 (20%) FLT3-ITD** [4]. RATIFY screened **3277 patients aged 18–59** with newly diagnosed AML; among the 717 FLT3-mutant patients randomised, strata were ITD-high (mutant:wild-type >0.7) 214, ITD-low (0.05–0.7) 341, TKD 162 [12]. Pediatric AML: **341/1857 (18.4%)** of TARGET patients FLT3-ITD-positive [7]. A meta-analysis of 20 prospective cohorts (n=10,970) held 1226 FLT3-TKD versus 9744 FLT3-wild-type patients [21].

Co-mutations, RATIFY genomic landscape (475 FLT3-mutant patients): *NPM1* 291 (61%), *DNMT3A* 187 (39%), *WT1* 100 (21%), *TET2* 55 (12%), *NRAS* 53 (11%), *RUNX1* 53 (11%), *PTPN11* 45 (10%), *ASXL1* 38 (8%). The *FLT3*/*NPM1*/*DNMT3A* triple genotype occurred in 157 (33%); ≥1 additional mutation in 463/475 (97.5%). *WT1* co-mutation was adverse for OS (HR 1.83, 95% CI 1.38–2.43, p<0.001) and *NPM1* favourable (HR 0.60, 95% CI 0.46–0.77, p<0.001), with *NPM1* largely abrogating the *WT1* effect [5]. Myelodysplasia-related gene (MRG) mutations co-occurred in 171/862 (20%) intensively treated FLT3-ITD patients in the HARMONY platform (4078 patients with molecular data) [9].

## Prognostic significance

**Allelic ratio.** In the MRC young-adult cohort, relapse risk and OS worsened progressively with rising FLT3-ITD mutant level (p<0.001 for both); even the lowest group (1–24% of total *FLT3* alleles) had significantly worse relapse risk than wild-type, and mutant level was the strongest multivariable predictor of relapse risk. Mutant size and number had no impact [22]. In PETHEMA (n=2901), allelic ratio >0.5 predicted lower CR rate and worse OS (HR 1.47, p=0.009) and >0.8 predicted worse RFS (HR 2.1, p<0.001); optimal data-derived cut-offs were 0.44 for OS and 0.8 for RFS [4]. ELN 2022 nonetheless **removed** allelic ratio, citing the difficulty of standardising the measurement, the availability of *NPM1* MRD, and the effect of midostaurin-based therapy [2].

**Modification by *NPM1*.** In PETHEMA, median OS among *NPM1*-mutated patients fell stepwise with FLT3-ITD burden: 34.3 months (ITD-negative), 25.3 months (ratio ≤0.25), 14.5 months (≤0.5), 10 months (≥0.5), p<0.001 [4]. In the same group's ELN comparison, 2-year OS was 75.1% for *NPM1*-mutated/ITD-wild-type versus 47.9% for *NPM1*-mutated/ITD-mutated [8]. HARMONY adds a modifier: MRG mutations were **not** independently prognostic in FLT3-ITD AML overall, and were rare (9%) and neutral in the *NPM1*-co-mutated subgroup, but in FLT3-ITD/*NPM1*-wild-type AML predicted shorter RFS (HR 1.37, 95% CI 1.01–1.88, p=0.046) and OS (HR 1.34, 95% CI 1.02–1.74, p=0.032), with survival comparable to ELN adverse risk [9]. The ELN 2022 rule assigning FLT3-ITD + MRG to adverse risk therefore rests on limited and partly discordant evidence.

**Insertion site.** In 452 RATIFY patients (908 ITDs), insertion site distributed as JMD-sole 251 (55%), JMD/TKD1 117 (26%), TKD1-sole 84 (19%), with estimated 4-year OS of 0.44, 0.50 and 0.30 respectively (p=0.032). TKD1-sole was independently unfavourable, and **midostaurin benefit was significant only in the JMD-sole group** [23]. In QuANTUM-First, longer insertion length correlated with worse outcome, but quizartinib benefit held irrespective of length [24].

**TKD.** Unlike ITD, FLT3-TKD has no consistent prognostic effect: pooled DFS HR 1.12 (95% CI 0.90–1.41) and OS HR 0.98 (95% CI 0.76–1.27) across 20 cohorts, with heterogeneity attributable to patient source [21]. TKD should not be treated as an adverse marker.

**Risk-group reclassification.** Applying ELN 2022 to 546 intensively treated PETHEMA patients moved 14.5% of fit patients to a different risk group: 3.7% from favourable to intermediate (20 patients with mutated *NPM1* and low-allelic-ratio FLT3-ITD) and 0.9% from adverse to intermediate (5 patients with high-allelic-ratio FLT3-ITD) [8]. In an Alliance cohort of 1637 adults with de novo AML treated 1986–2013 (**pre-FLT3-inhibitor**), ELN 2022 groups gave median OS 4.1 years / 5-year OS 48% (favourable, 35%), 0.9 years / 22% (intermediate, 24%), and 0.6 years / 7% (adverse, 41%) [25].

**Modification by FLT3 inhibitors.** The adverse prognosis is partially, not fully, reversible. RATIFY: OS HR 0.78 (one-sided p=0.009), EFS HR 0.78 (one-sided p=0.002), benefit consistent across ITD-high, ITD-low and TKD strata [12]. QuANTUM-First: median OS 31.9 versus 15.1 months, HR 0.78 (95% CI 0.62–0.98, p=0.032) [13]. A registry signal in the same direction — 3-year OS 85.2% with versus 54.8% without midostaurin among ELN 2022 intermediate-risk PETHEMA patients (p=0.04) — is observational and confounded [8].

## Diagnostic testing and MRD

**At diagnosis.** *FLT3* testing is mandatory for every newly diagnosed and every relapsed/refractory patient, and must be fast enough to inform day-8 start of a FLT3 inhibitor. A Canadian consensus recommends results in the electronic record **within 2–5 calendar days**, using PCR-fragment analysis for ITD (chosen for turnaround) and PCR-RFLP for D835/I836, with PCR sensitivity standardised to 1–5%; it also recommends abandoning "allelic ratio" and "variant allele frequency" in favour of the proportion of mutant to total alleles [26]. Capture-based NGS panels systematically under-call long ITDs, so an ITD-aware assay or pipeline is required. The FDA-approved companion diagnostic for all three licensed agents is the LeukoStrat CDx FLT3 Mutation Assay (Invivoscribe), detecting ITD plus D835 and I836 [13][14].

**Re-testing at relapse is not optional.** Because the lesion is clonally unstable (below), *FLT3* status must be re-established at every relapse regardless of the diagnostic result — that is how gilteritinib-eligible patients are found.

**MRD.** The 2025 ELN-DAVID consensus is the current standard and marks a substantive change: **ultra-high-sensitivity NGS-based FLT3-ITD MRD is now recommended for FLT3-ITD AML after intensive chemotherapy and before allogeneic HCT**, with a new qualitative framework (optimal / warning / high risk of treatment failure) [15]. The companion technical note specifies a limit of detection of at least 1×10⁻⁴ (0.01%, MRD4), DNA input ideally ≥700 ng (≥500 ng, ~75,000 cell equivalents), 8+20 (or 25) PCR cycles, 2×250 or 2×300 sequencing cycles, ≥3 identical mutant reads for a positive call, a pipeline capable of *de novo* ITD detection rather than only tracking the diagnostic sequence, reporting of every ITD with its VAF at every timepoint, and turnaround ≤3 weeks. Marrow ITD burden may be 5–10× higher than blood [16].

In a CIBMTR-based study, 1075 patients were sequenced; among the 451-patient validation cohort transplanted 2018–2019, the 78 (17.3%) with residual *NPM1* and/or FLT3-ITD variants in pre-transplant blood at VAF ≥0.01% had 3-year relapse of 68% versus 21% (HR 4.32, 95% CI 2.98–6.26, p<0.001) and 3-year survival 39% versus 63% (HR 2.43, 95% CI 1.71–3.45, p<0.001) [27]. In MORPHO, 42.2% of placebo-arm patients with detectable FLT3-ITD MRD relapsed versus 13.4% of those without; 14.8% had multiple FLT3-ITD clones and fared worse irrespective of arm [28]. In QuANTUM-First, quizartinib produced deeper FLT3-ITD MRD reduction than placebo, end-of-induction MRD correlated with relapse and survival, and marrow outperformed blood [24].

## Therapeutic implications

**Approved — midostaurin (Rydapt).** Initial US approval 2017, with cytarabine/daunorubicin induction and cytarabine consolidation, for newly diagnosed adults who are **FLT3 mutation-positive (ITD or TKD)**; 50 mg orally twice daily, days 8–21 of each induction and consolidation cycle; not indicated as single-agent induction; no boxed warning [29]. Evidence: RATIFY (phase 3 positive), n=717, ages 18–59 [12].

**Approved — quizartinib (Vanflyta).** FDA 20 July 2023; EU marketing authorisation 6 November 2023 [13][30][31]. Indication is restricted to **FLT3-ITD-positive** newly diagnosed AML, with induction (35.4 mg days 8–21), consolidation (35.4 mg days 6–19) and maintenance monotherapy (26.5 mg days 1–14, escalating to 53 mg if QTcF ≤450 ms, up to 36 cycles). **Boxed warning: QT prolongation, torsades de pointes and cardiac arrest**; available only through the Vanflyta REMS; contraindicated in severe hypokalaemia, severe hypomagnesaemia, long QT syndrome or history of ventricular arrhythmias; do not initiate if QTcF >450 ms; baseline and weekly ECGs. **Limitation of use: not indicated as maintenance after allogeneic HCT** [13]. Evidence: QuANTUM-First (phase 3 positive); the FDA review notes complete response in 55% of both arms but median duration of complete response of 38.6 versus 12.4 months [13][30].

A post hoc maintenance analysis supports and refines that limitation: no OS benefit was shown in patients who had received allo-HCT, whereas in non-transplanted patients quizartinib maintenance gave OS HR 0.401 (95% CI 0.192–0.838), and among those MRD-negative at maintenance onset OS HR 0.194 (95% CI 0.056–0.676); patients MRD-negative at completion of consolidation reached 89.1% (95% CI 70.0–96.4) 3-year survival on quizartinib maintenance without transplant. These analyses were post hoc and not powered [32].

**Approved — gilteritinib (Xospata).** Initial US approval 2018, for relapsed or refractory FLT3-mutated AML (ITD, D835, I836), 120 mg once daily; the companion diagnostic detects ITD plus D835 and I836. **Boxed warning: differentiation syndrome**; warnings include PRES, QT prolongation, pancreatitis and embryo-fetal toxicity [14]. Evidence: ADMIRAL (phase 3 positive), n=371 (247 gilteritinib versus 124 salvage chemotherapy): median OS 9.3 versus 5.6 months, HR 0.64 (95% CI 0.49–0.83, p<0.001); CR/CRh 34.0% versus 15.3%; CR 21.1% versus 10.5%. **Event-free survival did not reach significance**: 2.8 versus 0.7 months, HR 0.79 (95% CI 0.58–1.09) [33].

**Negative and failed programmes — recorded at full weight.**
- **QuANTUM-R** (phase 3, R/R FLT3-ITD, n=367): quizartinib versus salvage chemotherapy, median OS 6.2 versus 4.7 months, HR 0.76 (95% CI 0.58–0.98, p=0.02); treatment-emergent deaths 33% versus 17%. Neither the US nor the EU label covers relapsed/refractory disease — quizartinib's licensed indication is newly diagnosed FLT3-ITD-positive AML only [13][31][34].
- **LACEWING** (phase 3 negative): gilteritinib + azacitidine versus azacitidine in newly diagnosed FLT3-mutated AML unfit for intensive chemotherapy. Interim analysis median OS 9.82 versus 8.87 months, HR 0.916 (95% CI 0.529–1.585, p=0.753); **stopped for futility**, despite CRc 58.1% versus 26.5% (p<0.001). Final 6.5-year results: median OS 9.82 versus 9.23 months (p=0.182), and only 5.24 months for gilteritinib monotherapy; 2-year OS 18.8% / 12.9% / 4.5%; ORR 70.2% versus 36.8% [35][36]. A response benefit without a survival benefit.
- **MORPHO / BMT CTN 1506** (phase 3, primary endpoint not met): gilteritinib versus placebo as post-HCT maintenance in FLT3-ITD AML — RFS HR 0.679 (95% CI 0.459–1.005, two-sided p=0.0518). The MRD-positive subgroup showed RFS HR 0.515 (95% CI 0.316–0.838, p=0.0065) with no benefit in MRD-negative patients; this is a secondary/subgroup finding, not a met primary endpoint [28][37].
- **HOVON 156 AML / AMLSG 28-18 / PASHA** (NCT04027309, phase 3, primary endpoint not met): gilteritinib versus midostaurin with intensive chemotherapy in newly diagnosed FLT3-mutated AML. Astellas and HOVON confirmed on 9 March 2026 that the trial **did not meet its primary endpoint of overall survival**, with similar rates of treatment-emergent and grade ≥3 adverse events between arms [38]. Figures circulating from the EHA 2026 presentation (median OS not reached in either arm, HR 1.02, 95% CI 0.81–1.28, p=0.864; CR after induction 78.9% versus 83.3%; median EFS 51.1 versus 19.9 months) are **secondary conference coverage only** — no peer-reviewed publication was retrievable as of 9 September 2026, and these numbers should be treated as provisional.
- **SORMAIN** (phase 2 positive, drug not approved for AML): sorafenib versus placebo for 24 months post-HCT in FLT3-ITD AML, n=83; relapse-or-death HR 0.39 (95% CI 0.18–0.85, log-rank p=0.013); 24-month RFS 85.0% versus 53.3% [39].
- **COG AAML1031 sorafenib** (non-randomised, pediatric): MTD 200 mg/m²/day; 72 patients in cohorts 2/3 versus 76 historical high-allelic-ratio FLT3-ITD controls. Outcomes were superior with sorafenib and multivariable analysis accounting for transplant and favourable co-mutations confirmed benefit — but the comparator was non-randomised and sorafenib-treated patients were transplanted more often [40].
- **Azacitidine + venetoclax + gilteritinib triplet** (phase 1–2 signal): 30 unfit newly diagnosed FLT3-mutated patients, median age 71, 73% FLT3-ITD. CR/CRi 96%; median RFS 23.4 and OS 29.7 months; 3-year RFS 43%, OS 46%. FLT3-ITD subset: median RFS 17.0 and OS 21.8 months, 3-year 32% and 36%. 68% of those receiving ≥1 consolidation cycle needed a dose or duration reduction of at least one drug — a real tolerability cost. Baseline RAS-pathway mutation predicted worse outcome; randomised comparison is still needed [41].
- **QuANTUM-Wild** (NCT06578247, phase 3, ongoing, **no results**): quizartinib plus chemotherapy and as maintenance in newly diagnosed **FLT3-ITD-negative** AML, 2:2:1 randomisation across three arms, primary endpoint OS. Design publication only [42].
- **FLT3 CAR-T** (first-in-human, negative for efficacy): two patients with R/R FLT3⁺ AML; grade 1 CRS, FLT3⁺ blasts eliminated, CD34⁺ HSPCs preserved, but **neither patient achieved remission** because FLT3-negative blasts persisted [43].

**Transplant.** FLT3-ITD remains a frequent indication for allo-HCT in CR1, and in PETHEMA allo-HCT gave better OS and RFS than auto-HCT in *NPM1*/FLT3-ITD co-mutated AML regardless of allelic-ratio cut-off [4]. But the direction of travel — from MORPHO and the QuANTUM-First maintenance analysis — is toward **MRD-directed rather than genotype-directed** transplant and maintenance decisions [28][32].

## Resistance and relapse

**On-target.** Secondary activation-loop mutations, above all at D835, are the classical escape route from type II inhibitors, via stabilisation of the DFG-in conformation [3]. For the type I inhibitor gilteritinib the analogous lesion is the **F691L gatekeeper** substitution; in one series only 5/31 gilteritinib-treated patients acquired a secondary TKD mutation, 4 of 5 being F691L and all occurring at doses <200 mg — suggesting dose intensity matters [20].

**Off-target.** The dominant clinical mechanism of secondary resistance to selective FLT3 inhibition is **RAS/MAPK pathway activation** — usually emergent *NRAS* or *KRAS* mutations, less often *BCR-ABL1* fusions. Single-cell DNA sequencing showed RAS mutations arising within FLT3-mutant subclones, expansion of alternative FLT3-wild-type subclones, or both at once [44]. Reviews additionally implicate FLT3-ligand elevation, marrow-stromal CYP3A4, FGF2 and CXCR4 as adaptive/microenvironmental mechanisms — review-level synthesis, not trial evidence [45].

**Clonal instability** is the defining clinical peculiarity of this lesion. In the original paired-sample study, of 20 patients FLT3-mutant at diagnosis, **5 (all ITD-positive) had no detectable mutation at relapse** and one relapsed with a completely different ITD absent at presentation; 4 of 24 patients wild-type at diagnosis acquired a mutation at relapse. The authors concluded that FLT3 mutations are secondary, unstable events to be used cautiously as MRD markers [17]. The prospective non-interventional CLEVO study (650 enrolled; 166 tested at first relapsed/refractory event) found **~15% had gained or lost a *FLT3* mutation**, including 18/121 (14.9%) FLT3-negative-at-relapse patients who had been FLT3-positive at baseline [18]. Under FLT3-inhibitor pressure, loss is commoner still: in the aza/ven/gilteritinib cohort the FLT3 mutation was undetectable in 67% of evaluable relapses [41]. Multiple coexisting ITD clones occurred in 14.8% of MORPHO participants, with worse survival [28]. Consequences: (i) re-test *FLT3* at every relapse; (ii) never use FLT3-ITD as the sole MRD marker when a stable marker such as mutated *NPM1* exists; (iii) require *de novo* ITD calling in MRD pipelines [16][17].

## Open questions

1. **Should allelic ratio return?** ELN 2022 removed it on standardisation grounds, yet PETHEMA (n=2901) shows a clear dose–response while HARMONY found it did not further stratify FLT3-ITD/*NPM1*-wild-type AML [2][4][9]. Whether a standardised mutant-allele proportion adds anything to MRD is unresolved.
2. **Is the ELN 2022 assignment of FLT3-ITD + MRG to adverse risk correct?** HARMONY found MRG mutations not independently prognostic in FLT3-ITD AML overall, with an effect confined to the *NPM1*-wild-type subgroup [9].
3. **Insertion site is prognostic and predictive yet absent from every guideline.** TKD1-sole insertion was adverse and midostaurin benefit confined to JMD-sole insertions in RATIFY [23]; whether it should govern drug choice is untested prospectively.
4. **Which FLT3 inhibitor, upfront, for whom?** HOVON 156 found no OS advantage for gilteritinib over midostaurin [38], and quizartinib has never been compared head-to-head with either.
5. **Can MRD negativity substitute for transplant?** The QuANTUM-First maintenance signal (89.1% 3-year survival in MRD-negative, non-transplanted patients) is post hoc and hypothesis-generating [32]; a prospective MRD-directed randomisation is the obvious next trial.
6. **How should FLT3-TKD be treated?** It carries no consistent adverse prognosis [21], is excluded from the quizartinib label, and has never been a phase 3 trial's primary population.
7. **Triplet versus doublet in unfit patients.** LACEWING was negative [35][36] and the aza/ven/gilteritinib triplet is a single-arm phase 2 [41]; randomised data are absent.
8. **Targeting FLT3-negative relapse.** Because a large share of post-FLT3-inhibitor relapses are FLT3-negative [18][41], the problem is increasingly the FLT3-wild-type ancestral clone rather than FLT3 itself — and FLT3-directed immunotherapy has so far failed on antigen heterogeneity [43].
