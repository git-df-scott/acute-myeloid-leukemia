# Measurable Residual Disease in AML: Prognostic Everywhere, Actionable Almost Nowhere

> Measurable residual disease (MRD) is the closest thing AML has to an early readout of cure, and the field has spent two decades proving that it predicts relapse. It does: across 81 studies and 11,151 patients, MRD negativity carried a hazard ratio of 0.36 for death and 0.37 for relapse or death, with estimated 5-year overall survival of 68% versus 34% [3]. What the field has *not* done is show, in a randomized comparison, that acting on an MRD result changes survival. Every MRD-directed strategy with clinical data — MRD-guided transplant allocation [17,18,19], pre-emptive azacitidine [20], venetoclax for molecular relapse [23,24], intensified conditioning [13], post-transplant maintenance [15] — rests on single-arm designs, post hoc subgroups, or historical comparison. Meanwhile the assays disagree with each other (flow and sequencing concordant in barely half of cases [7,10,37]), the marker may be clonal hematopoiesis rather than leukemia [7,9], the lead time before morphologic relapse is often days [29,30], and the regulator that accepted MRD-negative remission as an accelerated-approval endpoint in myeloma in 2024 has explicitly *not* done so for AML, citing the disease's molecular heterogeneity [32,33]. MRD therefore sits precisely on the campaign's central fault line: the field can see the failure earlier than ever, and cannot yet prove it knows what to do about it.

## Key takeaways

- **MRD is the most robust post-diagnosis prognostic variable in AML, by any method and at any timepoint.** The 2020 meta-analysis (81 studies, N=11,151) estimated 5-year DFS of 64% vs 25% and OS of 68% vs 34% for MRD-negative vs MRD-positive patients, consistent across age, subtype, specimen, method and timing, with the sole exception of cytogenetic/FISH-based assessment [3]. A 2022 update (48 studies) confined to patients in morphologic CR found 5-year OS of 67% vs 31%, with the benefit of MRD negativity largest when the threshold was below 0.1% [4].
- **Every platform has a defined blind spot.** Multiparameter flow cytometry (MFC) reaches ~10⁻³ to 10⁻⁴ in most laboratories, is applicable to >90% of patients, and is the least standardized [1,2,36,37]; RT-qPCR for *NPM1* and CBF fusions reaches 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁶ but covers roughly 40% of adults [1,5,25,26]; error-corrected NGS applies to ~93% of patients with a suitable mutation at diagnosis but cannot by itself distinguish leukemia from clonal hematopoiesis [7,9,12]. In direct comparison NGS-MRD detected >80% of flow-positive cases while flow detected 49% of NGS-positive cases [10].
- **Persistent *DNMT3A*, *TET2* and *ASXL1* mutations are not MRD — usually.** In HOVON/SAKK, persistence of "DTA" mutations in CR was not associated with relapse, whereas non-DTA persistence gave a 4-year relapse rate of 55.4% vs 31.9% (HR 2.14) [7]. But an ALFA-0702 error-corrected analysis found persistent DTA mutations *were* associated with worse OS (HR 2.16) [11], and after transplant DTA and non-DTA mutations showed similar relapse kinetics [30]. The exclusion rule is a convention with counter-evidence, not a settled fact.
- ***NPM1* MRD is the best-validated single marker and already changes who is transplanted.** In NCRI AML17, persistent *NPM1*-mutant transcripts in blood after two cycles (15% of 346 patients) predicted 3-year relapse of 82% vs 30% (HR 4.80) and survival of 24% vs 75% [5]. In AML17/AML19 (N=737 in remission), transplant in first CR improved 3-year OS from 24% to 61% in MRD-positive patients (HR 0.39) and did nothing for MRD-negative patients (79% vs 82%, HR 0.82) — including those with *FLT3*-ITD [19]. ALFA-0702 found the same interaction (P=.024 for DFS) [17].
- **Pre-transplant MRD predicts post-transplant failure in every dataset, and MRD-positive remission behaves like active disease.** At a single myeloablative-transplant centre, 3-year relapse was 67% in MRD-positive CR, 65% with active disease, and 22% in MRD-negative CR; 3-year OS 26%, 23% and 73% [14]. In 822 CIBMTR patients with *FLT3*-ITD/*NPM1* AML, residual variants in pre-transplant blood (17.3%) gave 3-year relapse of 68% vs 21% (HR 4.32) and OS of 39% vs 63% [12]; residual *FLT3*-ITD carried a dose-dependent risk with no safe threshold [16].
- **The single best piece of evidence that MRD can be *acted on* is a post hoc analysis of a randomized trial, and it concerns conditioning intensity.** In BMT CTN 0901, patients with detectable pre-transplant mutations randomized to reduced-intensity conditioning relapsed at 67% vs 19% with myeloablative conditioning (3-year OS 43% vs 61%; RIC HR for relapse 6.38) [13]. This is the closest AML has to a randomized demonstration that an MRD-triggered decision changes survival; it was not the trial's primary question.
- **MRD-directed intervention trials exist, and none is randomized against an MRD-blind control.** GIMEMA AML1310 (N=500) used MRD to assign intermediate-risk patients to autologous vs allogeneic transplant and reported 2-year OS of 70% for MRD-positive patients sent to allograft versus 79% for MRD-negative patients sent to autograft [18]; RELAZA2 (N=53 treated) reported 58% relapse-free at 6 months after pre-emptive azacitidine for MRD positivity [20]; VALDAC (N=48) reported MRD-negative remission in 46% of molecular relapses treated with venetoclax + LDAC [23]; a 79-patient cohort in *NPM1* molecular failure reached MRD negativity in 71% [24]. Each is a single-arm study; none establishes that intervening early beats treating at hematologic relapse.
- **Lead time is the practical killer.** In core-binding-factor AML monitored every 3 months, 74% of relapses were not predicted by molecular monitoring and progressed from molecular to morphologic relapse in <100 days [29]. After transplant, an MRD marker became detectable in only 10%, 38% and 64% of patients at 6, 3 and 1 month before relapse — so quarterly monitoring would have caught 38% of relapses and monthly monitoring 64% [30].
- **MRD after venetoclax-based therapy is prognostic but its meaning is different.** In VIALE-A, 41% of composite responders reached flow MRD <10⁻³, with 12-month OS of 94% vs 67.5% for MRD ≥10⁻³ [21]; in *NPM1*-mutated patients on venetoclax combinations, marrow MRD negativity by cycle 4 gave 2-year OS of 84% vs 46% [22]. But the relapse reservoir after venetoclax is a differentiation state that flow may not resolve (see `04-venetoclax-bcl2-lower-intensity.md`), and menin inhibitors induce immunophenotypic switching in about half of patients [39], so a flow-negative result after differentiation therapy is a weaker claim than after cytotoxic therapy.
- **MRD is not an approvable surrogate in AML.** FDA's January 2020 guidance states that "the molecular heterogeneity of AML poses substantial challenges to the use of MRD as a biomarker", requires sponsors to show a marker "reflects the leukemia and not underlying clonal hematopoiesis", and prefers bone marrow and count-recovered CR as the substrate and timepoint [32]. In 2024 FDA accepted MRD-negative CR as an accelerated-approval endpoint in multiple myeloma — the first hematologic malignancy — on the back of trial-level meta-analyses that AML has not produced [33].
- **In the one trial that tested it directly, maintenance benefit was independent of MRD status.** QUAZAR AML-001 oral azacitidine improved OS and RFS whether or not patients were MRD-positive at baseline, converted 37% vs 19% from MRD-positive to negative, and extended MRD-negative duration by 6 months [27]; among *NPM1*-mutated MRD-positive patients median OS was 46.1 vs 10.0 months [28]. MRD identifies who is likely to relapse; it did not identify who benefits from this drug.

## 1. What is being measured, by which method, and how well

The 2018 ELN MRD Working Party consensus and its 2021 update are the reference standards for assay, threshold, timepoint and reporting [1,2]. The 2021 update added technical specifications for NGS-based MRD, integrative reporting across platforms, defined MRD response categories, and — importantly for this campaign — a section on MRD "as a prognostic and surrogate end point for drug testing" [2]. ELN 2022 incorporated "CR without MRD" (CR-MRD⁻) into the response criteria [8].

**Multiparameter flow cytometry.** MFC detects leukemia-associated immunophenotypes (LAIP) or "different-from-normal" (DfN) populations, usually with a positivity threshold of 0.1% of leukocytes. The HOVON/SAKK AML 42A study established its independent prognostic value in a cooperative-group setting: MRD >0.1% after cycle 2 predicted relapse after adjustment for consolidation and risk score [36]. A 10-centre French harmonization study (N=276) using a fixed CD45/CD34/CD33 backbone with CD117/CD13/CD7/CD15 found MRD negativity (<5×10⁻⁵) associated with better DFS and OS at every timepoint (all P<.0001) and prognostic within every cytogenetic group [37]. In NCRI AML17 (2,450 patients with prospective MFC-MRD), MRD positivity after course 1 carried the same outcome as morphologic partial remission (5-year OS 46% PR vs 51% MRD⁺ vs 70% MRD⁻), and for *NPM1*-wild-type standard-risk patients MRD ≥0.1% after course 2 was followed by relapse in 89% [6]. The limits are analytic and operational: sensitivity is typically 10⁻³ to 10⁻⁴, assay design remains laboratory-specific despite the ELN template [2,38], and regenerating marrow after venetoclax or differentiation therapy can shift phenotypes [39].

**Molecular MRD by RT-qPCR.** For *NPM1*-mutated and CBF AML, RT-qPCR quantifies transcripts with sensitivity to 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁶ and is the ELN-preferred method [2]. The AML17 *NPM1* series [5], the ALFA-0702 analysis (a <4-log reduction in peripheral-blood *NPM1* MRD after induction gave a relapse sub-hazard ratio of 5.83 and OS HR 10.99) [17], the AMLSG 09-09 series (3,733 marrow and 3,793 blood samples from 469 patients, with ≥3-log reduction and MRD negativity in both compartments associated with lower relapse) [25], the French CBF intergroup trial (<3-log reduction after first consolidation was the sole independent prognostic factor: 36-month relapse 54% vs 22%) [26], and the AMLSG t(8;21) series (post-therapy MRD negativity in marrow gave 4-year OS of 93% vs 70%) [27] together constitute the strongest prognostic literature in AML. DNA-based methods (qPCR, ddPCR, deep sequencing) correlate strongly with each other but are less sensitive than RT-qPCR; with optimized cut-offs results agreed in 95% of 201 analyses, yet RT-qPCR missed 10% of samples with detectable leukemic DNA and vice versa [35].

**Error-corrected NGS.** NGS extends molecular MRD to patients without a qPCR target. In 482 HOVON/SAKK patients, at least one mutation was detectable at diagnosis in 89.2%, and mutations persisted in CR in 51.4% of those [7]. Thol and colleagues found a trackable mutation in 93% of 116 pre-transplant patients across 24 genes; NGS-MRD positivity gave 5-year relapse of 66% vs 17% (HR 5.58) [9]. The Pre-MEASURE study applied centralized sequencing of banked pre-transplant blood from 1,075 CIBMTR patients [12,16]. In an Indian cohort of 201 patients, panel NGS-MRD was positive in 71% after induction and 40.9% after consolidation, and was prognostic in intermediate-risk disease [10]. The rate-limiting issues are the DTA problem (below), the absence of a consensus panel or threshold, and cost.

**Leukemia stem cell MRD.** Because relapse originates in a stem/progenitor compartment (see `02-disease-biology-genomics.md`, section 10), the HOVON102 add-on study measured CD34⁺CD38⁻ LSC frequency alongside conventional MRD in 242 CR patients. MRD-high/LSC-high patients had HR 3.62 for death and 5.89 for relapse, and the LSC measurement improved on qPCR *NPM1* MRD [31]. This is prospective evidence that conventional MRD under-measures the relapse reservoir; it has not been incorporated into any treatment algorithm.

**Peripheral blood versus marrow.** ELN and FDA both prefer marrow [2,32]. Blood is roughly one log less sensitive for *NPM1* transcripts but was used in AML17 with full prognostic effect [5]. For flow, a 2026 prospective feasibility study of 118 patients reported 98% concordance with marrow and 96% sensitivity [40], whereas a German series found only 73.7% concordance with MRD positivity less common in blood [41]. The blood-marrow question is important because monthly monitoring [30] is only realistic from blood.

## 2. The clonal-hematopoiesis confounder

The HOVON/SAKK NEJM study defined the current convention: persistent DTA mutations (*DNMT3A*, *TET2*, *ASXL1*) do not predict relapse within 4 years and should be excluded from NGS-MRD calls [7]. The 2015 Washington University study that started the field had already found that persistent leukemia-associated mutations in ≥5% of marrow cells at day 30 predicted relapse (24 of 50 patients) [42], and the MD Anderson analysis of 131 patients found that complete mutation clearance was associated with lower relapse (2-year CIR 24% vs 46%), with associations stronger once preleukemic genes were removed [43]. The problem is that the preleukemic clone is a real relapse reservoir in some patients (`02-disease-biology-genomics.md`, section 4), and the ALFA-0702 error-corrected analysis found that persistent DTA mutations *were* associated with worse OS (HR 2.16) and that persistence of two or more mutations of any kind carried a relapse HR of 3.71 [11]. FDA's guidance requires sponsors to demonstrate that a marker "reflects the leukemia and not underlying clonal hematopoiesis" and to characterize the false-negative rate from marker-negative relapse [32]. There is no validated way to do either at the level of an individual patient.

## 3. Prognostic evidence, by setting

**After intensive induction.** The meta-analytic estimates above [3,4] are dominated by intensively treated younger adults. Timepoint matters: in AML17, MRD after course 2 retained independent prognostic value after adjusting for course-1 response [6]; in HOVON/SAKK the decision-relevant timepoint was after cycle 2 [36]; in *NPM1* AML the earliest informative blood measurement is after induction [17] and the ELN endorses post-cycle-2 blood [2]. Kinetics of clearance matter independently of endpoint status: in the Indian NGS series, patients MRD-negative after induction fared better than those who became negative later [10].

**Before transplant.** Walter's 2011 series (99 myeloablative recipients) gave 2-year OS of 30.2% vs 76.6% and relapse of 64.9% vs 17.6% for MFC-MRD-positive vs -negative CR1 [44]; Araki extended this to show MRD-positive CR and active disease had the same outcome [14]. NGS gives the same signal [9,12,16]. Multiple analyses converge on a relapse rate of roughly two-thirds after transplant in MRD-positive remission.

**After venetoclax-based therapy.** VIALE-A: 164 of 190 composite responders evaluable; 41% reached MRD <10⁻³ with a 12-month OS of 94.0% versus 18.7 months median OS in MRD-positive responders (adjusted HR 0.285) [21]. *NPM1* molecular MRD in 76 patients on venetoclax combinations: 58% reached marrow negativity, and 22 patients who electively stopped therapy in MRD-negative remission had 2-year treatment-free remission of 88% [22]. A retrospective series of 29 patients in remission ≥12 months on venetoclax found a median treatment-free remission of 45.8 months among those who stopped, with MRD-negative CR among the factors favouring durable remission [45]. These are the data behind the currently untested proposition that MRD could define a stopping rule for indefinite venetoclax (`04-venetoclax-bcl2-lower-intensity.md`, barrier 8).

**In maintenance.** QUAZAR AML-001 is the only phase 3 with serial central flow MRD. Baseline MRD was strongly prognostic, oral azacitidine improved OS and RFS independent of MRD status, converted 37% vs 19% from positive to negative, and 24% of MRD responders converted after >6 months of treatment [27,28]. MORPHO (BMT CTN 1506), the randomized trial of post-transplant gilteritinib, is the mirror image: benefit was concentrated in patients with detectable peri-transplant *FLT3*-ITD MRD (42.2% relapse in the placebo arm with MRD vs 13.4% without), MRD at any level worsened RFS, and 14.8% of participants had multiclonal *FLT3*-ITD with worse survival regardless of arm [15]. Neither trial randomized *on* MRD; both show that the same drug can help MRD-positive patients without helping MRD-negative ones (gilteritinib) or help both (oral azacitidine).

## 4. The actionability gap

This is the section the rest of the corpus depends on, so it is stated bluntly. The following are the interventional uses of MRD with clinical data, in descending order of evidential strength:

1. **Conditioning intensity for MRD-positive patients.** Post hoc analysis of a randomized trial (BMT CTN 0901): myeloablative conditioning halved relapse and improved OS in NGS-MRD-positive patients, with no difference in MRD-negative patients [13]. Pre-MEASURE reproduced the direction of effect for *FLT3*-ITD (reduced-intensity conditioning without melphalan carried higher risk at any MRD level) [16]. **Evidence tier:** randomized trial, secondary analysis; the trial was not designed to test MRD-directed conditioning, and myeloablation is not available to most older patients.
2. **Transplant allocation by MRD.** ALFA-0702 [17], GIMEMA AML1310 [18,46] and AML17/AML19 [19] all show that transplant benefit is confined to MRD-positive patients in *NPM1* or intermediate-risk disease. **Evidence tier:** prospective cohorts with treatment-by-MRD interaction analyses; GIMEMA allocated by protocol but had no randomized MRD-blind comparator. No trial has randomized MRD-negative favourable/intermediate patients to transplant versus no transplant, and none is likely to.
3. **Post-transplant intervention.** Risk-stratification-directed DLI in 814 Chinese patients reduced relapse in MRD-positive recipients to the level of MRD-negative recipients [47]; RELAZA2 gave 58% relapse-free survival at 6 months for pre-emptive azacitidine [20]; gilteritinib maintenance benefited MRD-positive patients in MORPHO [15]. **Evidence tier:** non-randomized allocation (DLI), single-arm phase 2 (RELAZA2), randomized-trial subgroup (MORPHO).
4. **Treatment of molecular relapse.** VALDAC (46% MRD-negative remission in molecular relapse, 2-year OS 67%) [23] and the international *NPM1* molecular-failure cohort (71% MRD negativity, 2-year OS 67%) [24]. **Evidence tier:** phase 2 single-arm and retrospective. Both compare favourably with historical outcomes of hematologic relapse (`11-relapsed-refractory-and-survivorship.md`); neither was compared with deferred treatment.
5. **Escalation of induction/consolidation for MRD positivity.** No adult randomized trial located. Pediatric cooperative groups intensify by flow MRD as standard practice (`12-pediatric-aml.md`).
6. **Stopping therapy on MRD negativity.** Observational only [22,45]; the one randomized test of venetoclax deintensification did not use MRD as its trigger and failed non-inferiority (`04-venetoclax-bcl2-lower-intensity.md`).

The absence is structural, not accidental. A randomized MRD-directed strategy trial must either withhold intervention from MRD-positive patients (increasingly unacceptable to investigators given the prognostic data) or offer it to MRD-negative patients who are unlikely to benefit. The field has resolved this by not running the trial, which leaves the prognostic literature doing work it cannot do: a prognostic biomarker "informs about the natural history of the disease in that particular patient in the absence of a therapeutic intervention" [32]. It does not certify that the intervention triggered by it helps.

## 5. Lead time, monitoring cadence, and the cost of looking

The clinical value of MRD-guided pre-emption depends on detecting relapse early enough to act. For CBF AML monitored quarterly per guideline, 32 of 43 relapses (74.4%) were not predicted and progressed to morphologic relapse in under 100 days; pre-emptive intervention was possible in 11 patients (25.6%) [29]. The AMLSG t(8;21) series likewise reported "a very short latency from molecular to morphologic relapse" and virtually all relapses within a year of ending treatment [27]. After allogeneic transplant, error-corrected NGS in 74 relapsing patients found that quarterly monitoring would have detected 38% of relapses and monthly monitoring 64%, with signaling-gene mutations giving the shortest lead times [30]. Monthly marrow sampling is not tenable, which is why the blood-versus-marrow question [40,41] is not a technicality.

## 6. MRD as a regulatory endpoint

The FDA guidance of January 2020 is the operative document [32]. Its general framework distinguishes a prognostic biomarker from a surrogate endpoint, describes two routes to surrogate acceptance (drug-specific submission or formal qualification), and asks for meta-analytic evidence that treatment effects on the surrogate correspond to effects on the clinical outcome. Its AML-specific section is short and cautious: bone marrow preferred; CR with count recovery preferred as the timepoint; sponsors must show the marker reflects leukemia rather than clonal hematopoiesis and must describe the false-negative rate from marker-negative relapse; and for targeted therapies whose target is the MRD marker (IDH1, IDH2, FLT3 inhibitors), sponsors should justify not using additional markers "to avoid false-negative results when clonality changes" [32]. Contrast the ALL section, where FDA states it has accepted MRD <0.01% as supporting evidence of efficacy in relapsed/refractory disease [32]. Multiple myeloma reached accelerated-approval status for MRD-negative CR in 2024 after large trial-level meta-analyses and an ODAC vote [33]. No AML approval has used MRD as a primary basis; the FDA-reviewed menin-inhibitor registrations report MRD as a secondary descriptor (`05-menin-inhibitors.md`). The 2021 ELN update explicitly discusses MRD "as a prognostic and surrogate end point for drug testing" [2], and the 2020 meta-analysis concludes that its results "support MRD status as an end point that may allow for accelerated evaluation of novel therapies" [3] — but patient-level prognostic association is not trial-level surrogacy, and the AML trial-level analysis has not been published.

## 7. Access

MRD testing requires central laboratories, validated assays and repeat marrow sampling. Within the corpus, real-world evidence on how often it is performed is thin: no US or European population-level study of MRD testing rates was located (see evidence-trail notes). One signal from outside high-income settings is direct: in 282 Thai children with AML (2015–2019), lack of access to MRD testing was independently associated with death (adjusted HR 1.7) [34]. Menin-inhibitor eligibility, venetoclax stopping decisions and transplant allocation now all turn on molecular results, which makes assay access a determinant of who receives modern therapy (`01-epidemiology-classification-risk.md`, barrier 8; `14-access-equity-and-cost.md`).

## What is established vs contested

**Established.**
- MRD negativity by any validated method is associated with roughly a halving of relapse and mortality hazard across settings [3,4].
- *NPM1* transcript MRD in blood after two cycles, and CBF fusion transcript reduction after consolidation, are independent prognostic factors that outweigh baseline genetics [5,17,25,26,27].
- Pre-transplant MRD-positive remission carries relapse risk comparable to transplanting active disease [14], and myeloablative conditioning mitigates but does not remove it [13,16].
- Transplant in CR1 benefits *NPM1*-mutated patients who are MRD-positive and not those who are MRD-negative [17,19].

**Contested or unresolved.**
- Whether DTA-mutation persistence should be excluded from NGS-MRD [7 vs 11,30].
- Whether flow MRD after venetoclax or differentiation therapy carries the same meaning as after cytotoxic therapy [21,22,39].
- Whether peripheral blood can replace marrow for flow MRD [40,41].
- Whether any MRD-triggered intervention — pre-emptive therapy, conditioning escalation, maintenance selection — improves survival over an MRD-blind strategy; no randomized comparison exists.
- Whether MRD-negative CR could be validated as a trial-level surrogate in AML, as it has been in myeloma [33].

## Barriers to cure in this domain

1. **The field measures relapse risk, not cure.** MRD negativity at 10⁻³ to 10⁻⁵ is compatible with 10⁴ to 10⁶ residual leukemic cells, and the cells that regenerate disease are enriched in a stem/progenitor compartment that conventional MRD undercounts: adding CD34⁺CD38⁻ LSC quantification identified a subgroup with "an almost 100% treatment failure probability" that MRD alone did not [31]. A negative MRD result is a statement about assay sensitivity and marker choice, not about eradication [32].
2. **Prognostic strength has substituted for interventional proof.** Two decades of cohort data [3,4] have made MRD-directed practice standard without a single randomized MRD-blind comparison. The only randomized-trial evidence that an MRD-triggered decision changes survival is a post hoc conditioning analysis [13]. The cost is that the field cannot distinguish "MRD identifies patients who will relapse" from "MRD identifies patients whom intervention X will save" — and the QUAZAR and MORPHO data show those are different questions with different answers for different drugs [15,27].
3. **Assays disagree, and each has a population it cannot see.** NGS and flow were concordant in roughly half of positive calls [10]; flow-versus-molecular concordance was 58–65% in a German series [41]; RT-qPCR and DNA methods each missed ~10% of the other's positives [35]. A trial that uses one platform will misclassify a defined fraction of patients, in a direction that depends on the platform.
4. **The marker may not be the leukemia.** Persistent DTA mutations are excluded by convention [7], yet they are associated with worse survival in some series [11] and behave like leukemic markers after transplant [30]. Preleukemic clones are a genuine relapse reservoir that no current MRD rule captures (`02-disease-biology-genomics.md`, barrier 2). The regulator has made resolving this a precondition for using NGS-MRD in registration trials [32].
5. **Lead time is shorter than the response time of the health system.** Quarterly monitoring catches a quarter to a third of relapses before they become morphologic [29,30]; monthly monitoring, needed to catch two-thirds, requires blood-based assays whose equivalence to marrow is unresolved [40,41]. MRD-guided pre-emption is therefore currently a strategy for the minority of relapses with slow kinetics — disproportionately *NPM1*-mutated disease.
6. **The MRD literature is an intensive-chemotherapy literature.** The meta-analyses [3,4], the ELN thresholds [2], and the transplant-allocation evidence [17,18,19] derive from patients under 60–65 treated with cytarabine-anthracycline induction. The majority of incident AML is older and treated with venetoclax-based regimens, where MRD is prognostic [21,22] but where the relapse reservoir is a differentiation state rather than a genotype (`04-venetoclax-bcl2-lower-intensity.md`, barrier 2) and where phenotypic switching under differentiation therapy [39] undermines the flow assay.
7. **Without surrogate status, MRD cannot shorten the drug-development cycle it was meant to shorten.** Myeloma obtained accelerated-approval status for MRD-negative CR through trial-level meta-analysis [33]; AML has produced only patient-level associations [3,4]. Until a trial-level analysis is done — which requires pooling randomized trials with serial central MRD, of which QUAZAR is nearly the only example [27] — every AML registration remains anchored to response rate or survival (`13-trial-design-endpoints-and-regulation.md`).
8. **Access to MRD testing is itself a survival variable.** Where MRD testing is unavailable, risk-adapted therapy cannot be delivered, and the one study that measured this found an adjusted mortality hazard of 1.7 [34]. Every advance in MRD-directed care widens the gap between centres that can measure and centres that cannot.

## Open research questions

1. A randomized trial of MRD-directed versus MRD-blind post-remission strategy in *NPM1*-mutated AML (the marker with the best assay), with OS as the primary endpoint, is feasible in a cooperative-group setting and has not been run. What sample size and MRD-positive rate would power it, and is it ethically acceptable to investigators given [19]?
2. Can a trial-level surrogacy analysis for MRD-negative CR in AML be assembled from the randomized trials with central serial MRD (QUAZAR [27], VIALE-A [21], AMLSG 09-09 [25], MORPHO [15]), and what would it show?
3. Does adding LSC quantification [31] or a stemness score (`02-disease-biology-genomics.md`, section 7) to MRD change the treatment-by-MRD interaction for transplant benefit?
4. Is monthly blood-based NGS monitoring after transplant [30] deliverable, and does earlier detection translate into survival when combined with a defined pre-emptive intervention?
5. What is the meaning of flow MRD negativity after venetoclax or menin-inhibitor therapy given phenotypic switching [39], and should molecular or LSC-based MRD replace flow in those settings?
6. Should persistent DTA mutations be excluded from MRD calls [7], included [11], or handled by allele-fraction trajectory rather than presence [30]?
7. What proportion of newly diagnosed adults in the United States and Europe actually receive guideline-concordant MRD testing, and at what cost? No population-level study was located.
8. Can MRD define a venetoclax stopping rule [22,45], and would a randomized stop-versus-continue trial in MRD-negative *NPM1*/*IDH2*-mutated patients be acceptable to patients and regulators?

### Evidence-trail notes

- Every reference above was retrieved on 2026-09-09 through PubMed E-utilities (abstract read) or, for [32], as the FDA PDF itself, whose AML section (lines 428–456 of the document) is quoted directly. Reference [46] (GIMEMA 6-year update) has no abstract in PubMed; its content is cited only as the follow-up publication of [18], not for any figure.
- **Real-world MRD testing rates.** Searches for population-level or claims-based studies of how often adults with AML receive MRD testing in the United States or Europe returned nothing usable. The claim that MRD testing is incompletely delivered in practice is therefore supported here only by the pediatric Thai cohort [34]; this is a documented gap, not a finding of universal access.
- **Randomized MRD-directed strategy trials.** Searches for randomized trials comparing MRD-guided with MRD-blind management in adult AML located none. The pediatric cooperative-group trials that stratify on MRD are covered in `12-pediatric-aml.md`. If a randomized adult trial exists that these searches missed, it belongs here and its absence is an error to correct.
- **Cost.** No cost-effectiveness analysis of MRD testing in AML was located.
- **MRD after venetoclax by flow in older adults outside VIALE-A**, and **immunophenotypic shift under venetoclax** (as distinct from menin inhibitors [39]), were searched for and not retrieved; the venetoclax-specific differentiation-state argument rests on the biology in `04-venetoclax-bcl2-lower-intensity.md`.
- Reference [38] (ELN flow assay validation) is cited for the existence of the harmonization effort; its PubMed record carries no volume/page and the entry is reproduced as retrieved.
- Coverage of ALL, myeloma and CML MRD is deliberately confined to the contrast in [32,33].
