# TP53-Mutated, Adverse-Risk, Secondary and Therapy-Related AML: The Subgroups Where Nothing Cures

> Roughly one in ten adults with AML carries a *TP53* mutation, three-quarters of them with both alleles hit [2], and for them every frontline option produces the same result: median overall survival of about six months with intensive chemotherapy (6.5), venetoclax-azacitidine (6.2), or azacitidine alone (6.1), across 12 studies pooled in 2023 [4]. Complete remission rates differ — 43% with intensive therapy, 33% with venetoclax-HMA, 13% with HMA alone — and survival does not [4]. In the venetoclax-azacitidine registration trial, *TP53*-mutated patients with poor-risk cytogenetics gained remissions (composite CR 41% vs 17%) and no survival (5.2 vs 4.9 months) [6]; in a 314-patient ontogeny analysis venetoclax added nothing at all (5.7 vs 6.1 months) [7]. The three drugs developed specifically for this population all failed in randomized trials: eprenetapopt missed its phase 3 primary endpoint (CR 33.3% vs 22.4%, P=0.13) [18], magrolimab was stopped for futility in both ENHANCE-2 (OS 4.4 vs 6.6 months) [21] and ENHANCE-3 (10.7 vs 14.1 months, more fatal infections) [22], and sabatolimab's program was terminated after negative phase 2 and 3 results [23,24]. Allogeneic transplant cures a minority — 2-year OS 35.1% overall and 24.6% with 17p loss or a complex karyotype [28] — and a newly acquired *TP53* mutation appears in 15% of relapsed or refractory patients who started without one, most often after intensive therapy or transplant [11]. The rest of the adverse-risk category — *MECOM* rearrangement, monosomal karyotype, inv(3) [31,32,33] — and secondary and therapy-related disease [36,37,38] behave similarly, with the important exception that secondary-ontogeny AML without *TP53* does respond to venetoclax [7]. This is the part of the disease for which the campaign's premise is most literally true: the shortage is not of agents that produce responses but of anything that changes how long people live.

## Key takeaways

- **Allelic state, not the mutation alone, carries the prognosis — and in AML almost everyone is multi-hit.** In 3,324 MDS patients, one-third of *TP53*-mutated cases were monoallelic and two-thirds multi-hit; the associations with complex karyotype, few co-mutations and poor outcome were confined to multi-hit disease, and monoallelic patients did not differ from wild-type in outcome or treatment response [1]. In 2,200 AML/MDS-EB specimens, *TP53* mutations occurred in 10.5%, with median VAF 47%, biallelic status in 76%, and 2-year OS of 12.8% with no molecular feature — allelic state, mutation number, clone size, co-mutations — separating outcomes further; AML and MDS-EB did not differ, and the authors propose a single molecular entity [2]. In the CPX-351 pivotal trial, multi-hit *TP53* gave median OS 3.8 versus 7.0 months for single-hit [3]. Classification systems handle this differently (ICC: a standalone entity requiring VAF ≥10%; WHO-HAEM5: no separate AML entity), and `01-epidemiology-classification-risk.md` documents the consequences [8,9].
- **The three standard frontline regimens are equivalent in survival and none reaches a year.** Meta-analysis of 12 studies: CR 43% (intensive), 33% (venetoclax-HMA), 13% (HMA); median OS 6.5, 6.2 and 6.1 months; duration of response 3.5 and 5.0 months [4]. Ten-day decitabine plus venetoclax in 35 *TP53*-mutated patients: ORR 66% vs 89% for wild-type, 60-day mortality 26% vs 4%, median OS 5.2 vs 19.4 months (HR 4.67), and "comparable to historical results with 10-day decitabine alone" [5]. A 358-patient comparison of intensive chemotherapy versus HMA-venetoclax in very-high-risk cytogenetics found identical composite CR (55% vs 54%) and no OS difference in *TP53*-mutated disease (8.1 vs 5.8 months, P=.17) [10].
- **Venetoclax's benefit is real in secondary-ontogeny AML and absent in *TP53*-mutated AML.** In 314 newly diagnosed patients, HMA+venetoclax versus HMA improved OS in secondary-ontogeny disease (14.1 vs 6.9 months, cCR 61% vs 18%, transplant 24% vs 6%) and not in *TP53*-mutated disease (5.7 vs 6.1 months, cCR 33% vs 37%) [7]. The pivotal-trial analysis is concordant: poor-risk cytogenetics with wild-type *TP53* gained CRc 70% vs 23% and OS 23.4 vs 11.3 months; with mutant *TP53*, CRc 41% vs 17% and OS 5.2 vs 4.9 months [6]. In 413 MD Anderson patients with *TP53*-aberrant myeloid neoplasms, venetoclax raised response rates without improving survival [12].
- **The 10-day decitabine signal did not become a treatment.** The 2016 study that made *TP53* AML famous for responsiveness found blast clearance in 21 of 21 *TP53*-mutated patients on 10-day decitabine, with robust but incomplete mutation clearance — and stated in the same abstract that the responses "were not durable" [13].
- **Every *TP53*-directed or *TP53*-enriched randomized program has failed.** Eprenetapopt (APR-246): phase 1b/2 and phase 2 single-arm studies reported ORR 62–71% and CR 44–47% in MDS, with far lower CR in AML (17% in the French series; 0% above 30% blasts) [14,15]; the phase 3 in *TP53*-mutant MDS (N=154) reported CR 33.3% vs 22.4% with azacitidine alone, P=0.13, by company press release on 28 December 2020 [18]; the combined 100-patient phase 2 follow-up gives median OS 11.8 months, with allograft after NGS clearance the only route to 2-year survival of 54% [16]. Magrolimab: phase 1b CR 32.2% and median OS 9.8 months in *TP53*-mutant AML [20]; ENHANCE-2 (*TP53*-mutated, untreated) OS HR 1.132 in the non-intensive arm (4.4 vs 6.6 months) and 1.434 in the intensive arm (7.3 vs 11.1 months), terminated for futility [21,25]; ENHANCE-3 (N=378, unselected unfit AML) OS HR 1.178 with fatal adverse events 19.0% vs 11.4% [22]. Sabatolimab: STIMULUS-MDS1 (N=127) missed both primary endpoints (CR 22% vs 18%, PFS HR 0.75) [23] and STIMULUS-MDS2 (phase 3, N=530) was terminated after failing its primary objective [24].
- **Transplant is the only intervention with a survival signal, and genotype sets its ceiling.** In 780 EBMT patients allografted in CR1, 2-year OS was 35.1% with a *TP53* mutation versus 64% without; 65.2% in the 29.6% of mutated patients without 17p loss or complex karyotype and 24.6% in the rest [28]. In 1,514 CIBMTR MDS patients, *TP53* mutations (19%) predicted shorter survival and time to relapse regardless of conditioning intensity [27]. In the MD Anderson cohort, transplant was independently associated with survival in *TP53*-aberrant AML [12]; in the five-centre very-high-risk-cytogenetics cohort, allograft carried an OS hazard ratio of 0.42 [10]. Post-transplant eprenetapopt-azacitidine maintenance (N=33) gave 1-year RFS 59.9% and OS 78.8% in a single-arm phase 2 [17].
- **The mutation is acquired under treatment, not only present at diagnosis.** Among 200 *TP53*-wild-type patients who relapsed or were refractory, 29 (15%) had a newly detectable *TP53* mutation — 23% after intensive therapy versus 10% after lower-intensity therapy, and 36% after transplant versus 12% without — with median OS of 4.6 months after acquisition [11]. Mutant *TP53* remained detectable in 73% of complete remissions by deep sequencing, and its detection was not associated with survival [2].
- **The other adverse-risk lesions have moved no further.** Monosomal karyotype gives 4-year OS of 3–4% in patients aged 15–60 [31]; inv(3)/t(3;3) carries monosomy 7 in 66% of cases and independently predicts relapse (HR 1.99) and death (HR 1.40) [32]; in 152 *MECOM*-rearranged patients, composite CR was 50% with intensive and 48% with low-intensity therapy, median OS 9 months at ≥20% blasts, and intensive therapy carried a hazard ratio of 2.0 for death [33]. *ASXL1*-mutated disease, reclassified as favourable for lower-intensity therapy by ELN 2024, behaved as intermediate-risk in 554 patients [34].
- **Secondary and therapy-related AML are a large fraction with age-dependent prognosis and a transplant-responsive minority.** In the Swedish registry, 18.7% of adult AML followed an antecedent hematologic disorder and 7.7% was therapy-related; both were independent risk factors for death (HR 1.51 and 1.72), with a large effect in younger patients and none in the elderly [37]. In 2,853 AMLSG patients, t-AML (7.0%) carried more adverse and monosomal karyotypes and predicted death in remission in younger intensively treated patients [36]. Among intensively treated Swedish patients, transplant was performed in 17% with AHD-AML and 20% with t-AML; 5-year OS in secondary AML in CR1 was 48% with transplant versus 20% with chemotherapy consolidation after propensity matching; no untransplanted post-MPN patient survived to 5 years [38]. Blast-phase MPN has 1- and 3-year survival below 20% and 5% without transplant [39].
- **Therapy-related disease is partly a clonal-hematopoiesis story and partly an exposure story.** Radiation, platinum and topoisomerase II inhibitors select for DNA-damage-response clones (*TP53*, *PPM1D*, *CHEK2*), and where CH had been detected before, the same mutation was present at t-MN diagnosis [35]. PARP inhibitors carried a pooled MDS/AML incidence rate ratio of 1.32 (95% CI 0.78–2.26) overall, but 5.43 in front-line use and 2.43 in unrestricted populations across 14 randomized trials [40].

## 1. *TP53*: allelic state, classification, and why the biology is so unforgiving

*TP53* mutation in myeloid disease is a two-state phenomenon. The analysis of 3,324 MDS patients established that multi-hit status — a second mutation, a 17p deletion, or copy-neutral loss of heterozygosity — is what carries the complex karyotype, the paucity of co-mutations, the high-risk presentation, and the risk of death and leukemic transformation, independent of IPSS-R; monoallelic patients behaved like wild-type [1]. In AML the distinction matters less because nearly everyone is multi-hit: 76% biallelic in the HOVON series, with median VAF 47% [2]. The CPX-351 pivotal-trial resequencing put 2-year OS at 7% for *TP53*-AML against 19% for myelodysplasia-related, 37% for other, and 70% for *DDX41*-mutated AML, and found the drug's survival benefit confined to myelodysplasia-related disease with none in *TP53*-AML [3]. Within the ELN 2022 framework, Fleming and colleagues report the frequency of *TP53* co-mutation within each risk component and the relevance of VAF below 10% [8]; the ICC's 10% VAF floor for the standalone entity [9] is an operational choice whose consequences `01-epidemiology-classification-risk.md` (barrier 4) discusses.

The biological reason for chemoresistance is not fully captured by "loss of p53-dependent apoptosis." `02-disease-biology-genomics.md` (section 2) describes the ongoing aneuploidy licensed by del(5q) and *TP53* loss and the expansion of *TP53*-mutant clones under cytotoxic selection; the 15% rate of newly acquired *TP53* mutations in relapsed/refractory disease, highest after intensive therapy and transplant [11], is that selection observed clinically. Persistence of the mutant clone through remission is the rule (73% of CR samples), and its detection in remission did not predict survival [2] — an MRD marker that is usually positive and, so far, uninformative (`09-measurable-residual-disease.md`). The immune microenvironment is also different: *TP53*-mutated marrows show higher *IFNG*, *FOXP3*, checkpoint and senescence gene expression [26], and *TP53*-deficient AML cells suppress T-cell-engager cytotoxicity through a TGF-β1-containing secretome [30] — findings that cut both ways for immunotherapy (`07-immunotherapy-antibodies-and-cellular.md`).

## 2. Outcomes with each current frontline regimen

The evidence tier for every statement in this section is retrospective, subgroup, or single-arm; no randomized trial confined to *TP53*-mutated AML has produced a positive result.

**Intensive chemotherapy.** Pooled CR 43%, median OS 6.5 months, EFS 3.7 months [4]. The very-high-risk-cytogenetics comparison (N=358; complex karyotype 90.2%, monosomal 64%, inv(3) 9.8%, *TP53* 51%) found composite CR 55% with intensive therapy versus 54% with HMA-venetoclax, no OS difference in patients aged 60–75 (7.7 vs 6.6 months) or in *TP53*-mutated disease (8.1 vs 5.8 months), and no association of frontline regimen with OS on multivariable analysis (HR 0.84) [10]. CPX-351 — the only intensive regimen approved specifically for secondary and therapy-related AML — improved survival only in myelodysplasia-related AML (9.7 vs 6.8 months) and not in *TP53*-AML in the resequenced pivotal trial [3].

**Hypomethylating agents.** CR 13%, median OS 6.1 months [4]. The 10-day decitabine study reported blast clearance in 100% of *TP53*-mutated patients versus 41% of wild-type, with survival similar to intermediate-risk patients on the same regimen and responses that were not durable [13]. Ten-day decitabine plus venetoclax did not improve on that history [5].

**Venetoclax-HMA.** CR 33%, CR/CRi 49%, median OS 6.2 months, duration of response 5.0 months [4]. The VIALE-A/phase 1b pooled analysis of poor-risk cytogenetics [6], the Dana-Farber ontogeny cohort [7], and the MD Anderson *TP53*-aberration cohort [12] agree: venetoclax raises response rates and does not lengthen survival in *TP53*-mutated AML, while producing large gains in poor-risk-cytogenetic and secondary-ontogeny disease without *TP53*. Toxicity was not the explanation — adverse-event profiles were similar between *TP53*-mutant and wild-type patients [6]. Sixty-day mortality of 26% on DEC10-VEN [5] shows that early death, not only relapse, limits the regimen here.

## 3. Investigational agents: the record

**p53 reactivators.** Eprenetapopt's single-arm data were among the most striking in myeloid disease: ORR 71% and CR 44% in 55 patients (73% ORR and 50% CR in MDS; 64% and 36% in AML with 20–30% blasts) with complete molecular remission in 38% [14]; ORR 62% and CR 47% in French MDS patients, but ORR 33% and CR 17% in AML, with 0% CR above 30% blasts [15]. The randomized phase 3 in *TP53*-mutant MDS (N=154, eprenetapopt-azacitidine versus azacitidine) reported CR 33.3% versus 22.4%, P=0.13, and did not meet its primary endpoint; the company stated it expected to present the data at a future conference [18] — no peer-reviewed publication of the phase 3 was located (see evidence-trail notes). The combined phase 2 long-term follow-up (N=100) gives ORR 69%, CR 41%, median CR duration 10.2 months, median OS 11.8 months, and 2-year OS of 54% in patients transplanted after CR with NGS negativity [16]. The venetoclax-azacitidine-eprenetapopt triplet (N=49) reported ORR 64% and CR 38% with one treatment-related death [19]; post-transplant maintenance (N=33) gave 1-year RFS 59.9% [17]. **Evidence tier: phase 3 negative on primary endpoint; phase 2 signals.**

Rezatapopt (PC14586), a Y220C-specific reactivator, produced a 20% overall response in 77 heavily pretreated solid-tumour patients (30% in *KRAS*-wild-type tumours at ≥1,150 mg) in its phase 1 [41]. In *TP53*-Y220C AML models it restores wild-type conformation and transcriptional activity but induces little apoptosis on its own because reactivated p53 induces MDM2 and XPO1 and fails to engage BCL-2; venetoclax fully compensated in vitro and in xenografts [42]. A 24-patient phase 1b with azacitidine in Y220C myeloid malignancies opened in January 2025 [43]. **Evidence tier: preclinical in AML; phase 1 recruiting.** Y220C is one hotspot among many; this approach addresses a fraction of *TP53*-mutated AML.

**Macrophage checkpoint (CD47).** Magrolimab-azacitidine phase 1b (N=87, 82.8% *TP53*-mutant, median VAF 61%): CR 32.2%, median OS 9.8 months in *TP53*-mutant patients [20]. ENHANCE-2 randomized untreated *TP53*-mutated patients to magrolimab-azacitidine versus venetoclax-azacitidine (non-intensive) or 7+3 (intensive); the interim non-intensive OS hazard ratio of 1.191 met the futility boundary; final OS 4.4 versus 6.6 months (HR 1.132) and 7.3 versus 11.1 months (HR 1.434); grade ≥3 infections 50.0% versus 53.1% [21,25]. ENHANCE-3 (magrolimab or placebo added to venetoclax-azacitidine in unselected unfit AML, N=378): OS 10.7 versus 14.1 months, CR 41.3% versus 46.0%, fatal adverse events 19.0% versus 11.4% driven by grade 5 infections (11.1% vs 6.5%) [22]. **Evidence tier: phase 3 negative (two trials).**

**TIM-3 (sabatolimab).** STIMULUS-MDS1 (randomized phase 2, N=127, higher-risk MDS) missed CR (22% vs 18%) and PFS (11.1 vs 8.5 months, HR 0.75) [23]; STIMULUS-MDS2 (phase 3, N=530) was terminated in December 2023 after failing its primary objective, with the sponsor stating the termination was not for safety [24]. **Evidence tier: phase 2 and phase 3 negative.**

**Immunotherapy signals.** Among 15 relapsed/refractory patients with *TP53* abnormalities treated with the CD123×CD3 DART flotetuzumab, 7 (47%) achieved complete responses, associated with higher baseline inflammation, CD8 and PD-1 gene expression [26]; `07-immunotherapy-antibodies-and-cellular.md` covers the program's status. Against that, *TP53*-deficient AML suppresses T-cell proliferation and cytokine secretion under a CD33 BiTE through a TGF-β1-mediated secretome [30]. Both findings are laboratory or early-phase.

## 4. Transplant

Transplant is the only treatment with a survival association in *TP53*-mutated AML, and the association is modest and heterogeneous. EBMT (N=780 in CR1): 2-year OS 35.1% versus 64% without the mutation; 65.2% in the minority without 17p loss or complex karyotype, 24.6% with either [28]. CIBMTR MDS (N=1,514): *TP53* mutations in 19%, shorter survival and time to relapse after adjustment, with the adverse effect similar under reduced-intensity and myeloablative conditioning — unlike RAS-pathway mutations, whose relapse effect appeared only with reduced intensity [27]. Two cohorts with untransplanted comparators found transplant independently favourable (MD Anderson multivariable analysis [12]; very-high-risk cytogenetics HR 0.42 [10]), with the selection bias inherent in who reaches transplant. The eprenetapopt long-term follow-up ties post-transplant survival to NGS clearance before transplant (2-year OS 54% in CR/NGS-negative transplanted patients) [16]; the acquisition of new *TP53* mutations after transplant in 36% of *TP53*-wild-type relapses [11] shows the graft's selective pressure runs both ways. No randomized trial of a *TP53*-specific pre-transplant or post-transplant strategy has reported; eprenetapopt-azacitidine maintenance is single-arm [17]. Transplant attrition and post-transplant relapse mechanisms are in `08-transplant-post-remission-and-maintenance.md`.

## 5. Other adverse-risk lesions

**Monosomal karyotype.** In 1,975 HOVON/SAKK patients aged 15–60, two or more autosomal monosomies, or one monosomy with a structural abnormality, defined a group with 4-year OS of 3–4%, outperforming complex karyotype as a predictor [31]. **inv(3)/t(3;3) and other 3q abnormalities.** In 6,515 HOVON/SAKK/AMLSG patients, 3q abnormalities occurred in 4.4%; inv(3)/t(3;3) (32% of them) carried monosomy 7 in 66% and independently predicted relapse (HR 1.99) and death (HR 1.40), enhanced by monosomy 7 [32]. **MECOM rearrangement.** In 152 MD Anderson patients, composite CR was 50% with intensive and 48% with low-intensity therapy; median OS was 17 months with <20% blasts and 9 months with ≥20%; intensive therapy carried a multivariable hazard ratio of 2.0 for death, and the authors suggest low-intensity approaches [33]. In the very-high-risk cohort, inv(3)/t(3;3) had a composite CR of 36% versus 67% for complex/monosomal karyotype and an independent OS hazard ratio of 2.12 [10]. **KMT2A rearrangement** is covered in `05-menin-inhibitors.md` and `reference/genes/kmt2a-nup98-fusions.md`. **Secondary-type mutations** (*SRSF2*, *SF3B1*, *U2AF1*, *ZRSR2*, *ASXL1*, *EZH2*, *BCOR*, *STAG2*) define the secondary ontogeny whose venetoclax responsiveness is the one good-news item in this file [7]; *ASXL1*'s reclassification as favourable under ELN 2024 was contradicted in 554 lower-intensity-treated patients, in whom it behaved as intermediate-risk on every backbone [34].

## 6. Secondary and therapy-related AML

Population frequencies come from Sweden: 73.6% de novo, 18.7% AHD-AML, 7.7% t-AML among 3,363 adults; CR rates lower and early death similar in secondary AML; survival hazard ratios 1.51 and 1.72, with the effect concentrated in younger patients and absent in the elderly [37]. The AMLSG series (t-AML 7.0% of 2,853) found more adverse and monosomal karyotypes, fewer *NPM1* and *FLT3*-ITD mutations, shorter latency after intercalating agents and topoisomerase II inhibitors, and an adverse effect on death in remission rather than relapse in younger intensively treated patients — the authors attribute it to cumulative toxicity [36]. Transplant delivered 5-year OS of 48% versus 20% for chemotherapy consolidation in secondary AML in CR1 after propensity matching, and was performed in only 17–20% of intensively treated secondary-AML patients [38]. Blast-phase MPN is the extreme case: 1- and 3-year survival below 20% and 5% with any chemotherapy, versus >30% 3-year survival with transplant, and no untransplanted Swedish post-MPN patient alive at 5 years [38,39].

The origin of t-MN is now partly traceable. Cancer therapy reshapes the fitness landscape of clonal hematopoiesis: radiation, platinum and topoisomerase II inhibitors select DNA-damage-response clones (*TP53*, *PPM1D*, *CHEK2*), sequential sampling shows these clones outcompeting others under therapy, and pre-existing CH mutations were present at t-MN diagnosis [35]. PARP inhibitors, whose use is expanding, carried a pooled MDS/AML incidence rate ratio of 1.32 (non-significant) across 14 randomized trials, but 5.43 in front-line use and 2.43 in populations not restricted to *BRCA* carriers [40]. Trends in t-AML incidence, and the clonal-hematopoiesis link, are in `01-epidemiology-classification-risk.md` and `02-disease-biology-genomics.md` (sections 2 and 4).

## What is established vs contested

**Established.**
- *TP53*-mutated AML is overwhelmingly multi-hit and has median OS of about 6 months with every standard regimen [2,4].
- Venetoclax adds response but not survival in *TP53*-mutated AML, while adding both in secondary-ontogeny and poor-risk-cytogenetic *TP53*-wild-type AML [6,7,12].
- Eprenetapopt (phase 3), magrolimab (two phase 3s) and sabatolimab (phase 2 and 3) have failed [18,21,22,23,24].
- Transplant is associated with survival, with 2-year OS around 35% overall and around 25% with 17p loss or complex karyotype [28].
- *TP53* mutations are acquired under therapy in a substantial minority of relapses [11].

**Contested or unresolved.**
- Whether any subgroup of *TP53*-mutated AML (single-hit, low VAF, no complex karyotype, Y220C) is biologically distinct enough to respond to a targeted or transplant-based strategy [1,2,28,42].
- Whether intensive chemotherapy retains any role in *TP53*-mutated AML given equivalent survival and higher toxicity [4,10].
- Whether the flotetuzumab responses in *TP53*-abnormal disease [26] reflect a durable immunotherapeutic opening or a small-cohort signal.
- Whether the *TP53* immune microenvironment [26] and secretome [30] make T-cell therapies more or less viable here.
- The true incidence of t-MN after newer exposures (PARP inhibitors, radioligands, CAR-T lymphodepletion) and whether CH screening before therapy can prevent it [35,40].

## Barriers to cure in this domain

For *TP53*-mutated AML specifically, a cure would have to do something no current agent does. Stated as requirements, with the evidence that each is unmet:

1. **Kill a clone that does not need p53 to survive chemotherapy and expands under it.** Blast clearance is achievable — 100% with 10-day decitabine [13], 41–43% CR with venetoclax-HMA or intensive therapy [4] — but the clone persists in 73% of remissions [2] and regrows within months [4,13]. Response rate is the wrong readout here more clearly than anywhere else in AML: three regimens with CR rates of 13%, 33% and 43% deliver identical survival [4].
2. **Do it without killing the patient.** Sixty-day mortality of 26% on decitabine-venetoclax [5] and fatal infections of 11.1% on the magrolimab triplet [22] show that the therapeutic window is narrow in a population that is older, often previously treated, and cytopenic. Any candidate must improve survival at a toxicity no greater than azacitidine's.
3. **Address aneuploidy, not a single lesion.** Multi-hit *TP53* is a chromosomal-instability state [1,2] and the complex karyotype, not the mutation, determines transplant outcome [28]. Reactivating p53 (eprenetapopt, rezatapopt) or unmasking phagocytosis (magrolimab) treats one consequence of that state; the negative phase 3s [18,21,22] are evidence that single-mechanism approaches are insufficient, not merely that particular molecules were weak.
4. **Reach transplant and survive it.** Transplant is the only intervention with a survival association [10,12,28], and it is reached by a minority, in remission by fewer, and with NGS clearance — the condition associated with 2-year survival of 54% [16] — by fewer still. A candidate therapy's most plausible cure-relevant role is as a bridge that produces NGS-negative remission; that endpoint has not been used to select agents.
5. **Prevent the mutation from being acquired.** Fifteen percent of *TP53*-wild-type relapses have acquired a *TP53* mutation, concentrated after intensive therapy and transplant [11]. Any frontline strategy that increases cure in *TP53*-wild-type AML by cytotoxic intensification carries a documented cost in this direction, and no trial reports acquired *TP53* mutation as an endpoint.
6. **Run trials that can detect a survival difference in a population with median OS of 6 months.** ENHANCE-2 is the model: a randomized, *TP53*-selected, OS-primary trial with a venetoclax-azacitidine comparator, terminated at interim futility [21]. It shows the design is feasible and that response-rate-based phase 2 signals [20] do not predict its result. The corpus contains no *TP53*-selected randomized trial with a positive result and no ongoing phase 3 identified in this file (`15-models-pipeline-and-translation.md` enumerates the pipeline).
7. **For the wider adverse-risk category, distinguish who is truly untreatable.** *TP53*-wild-type poor-risk cytogenetics [6], secondary ontogeny [7], *MECOM* rearrangement with <20% blasts [33], and *TP53*-mutated AML without 17p loss or complex karyotype [28] all have materially better outcomes than the category average with current therapy. Treating "adverse" as one indication, as `01-epidemiology-classification-risk.md` (barrier 3) argues, misallocates transplant and trial slots in both directions.
8. **Prevent secondary and therapy-related disease rather than treat it.** The clonal-hematopoiesis-to-t-MN path is mechanistically described [35] and exposure-specific risks are quantifiable [40]; no prospective interception trial in therapy-exposed CH carriers was located.

What evidence would show a candidate is on the way: a randomized trial in *TP53*-mutated AML with an OS primary endpoint and a venetoclax-azacitidine comparator; a transplant-bridging rate and pre-transplant NGS-negativity rate reported prospectively; and a demonstration that the agent's activity is not confined to single-hit or non-complex-karyotype disease.

## Open research questions

1. Is there a *TP53*-mutated subgroup — single-hit, low-VAF, non-complex, or hotspot-specific — in which any current regimen or transplant reliably cures, and should trials stratify or select on it [1,2,28]?
2. Does rezatapopt-venetoclax reproduce in patients the apoptosis restoration seen in Y220C models [42,43], and what fraction of *TP53*-mutated AML carries a druggable hotspot?
3. Why did magrolimab's phase 1b signal [20] fail to translate in two randomized trials [21,22] — patient selection, drug effect, infection-driven mortality, or all three — and what does that imply for other phagocytosis-checkpoint agents?
4. Can pre-transplant NGS clearance of *TP53* [16] be made a prospective trial endpoint and a selection criterion for transplant?
5. What is the incidence of acquired *TP53* mutation per frontline regimen [11], and should it be a reported safety endpoint in intensive-therapy trials?
6. Does the *TP53* immune microenvironment [26,30] identify patients who benefit from T-cell engagers or checkpoint blockade, and can the TGF-β1 axis be targeted?
7. Should *MECOM*-rearranged and inv(3) AML be treated with low-intensity therapy by default [33], and is there a transplant-bridging strategy specific to them?
8. Can therapy-exposed patients with DNA-damage-response clonal hematopoiesis [35] be identified before t-MN develops, and is there an intervention worth randomizing?

### Evidence-trail notes

- All journal references were retrieved on 2026-09-09 through PubMed E-utilities with abstracts read; [18], [24], [25] and [43] are the press release and registry records named, retrieved directly.
- **The eprenetapopt phase 3 has no located peer-reviewed publication.** Its result is cited from the sponsor's press release [18], which is a secondary source by this repository's rules; the figures are the sponsor's and were not independently verified. If a publication exists that these searches missed, it should replace [18].
- **Ciurea et al. (Blood 2018), a widely cited single-centre transplant series in *TP53*-mutated AML/MDS, carries no abstract in PubMed and was not read; it is not cited.** The transplant figures here rest on [10,12,27,28,38].
- Reference [38] duplicates [29] because the same Swedish transplant analysis is cited for two separate claims; both entries are retained so that numbering stays contiguous — a future edit should merge them.
- **WHO-HAEM5** is not cited here for the classification contrast; the ICC definition [9] and the comparison are handled in `01-epidemiology-classification-risk.md`, whose references were verified in the corpus's mechanical pass.
- Regulatory status of CPX-351, venetoclax and azacitidine is not restated here; see `03-intensive-chemotherapy-backbone.md` and `04-venetoclax-bcl2-lower-intensity.md`.
- Therapy-related AML after lenalidomide, autologous transplant, radioligand therapy and CAR-T lymphodepletion was not retrieved and is not characterized; the PARP-inhibitor meta-analysis [40] is the only exposure-specific quantification here.
- No ongoing phase 3 trial confined to *TP53*-mutated AML was retrieved in this file's searches; that absence is stated as a barrier and should be re-checked against `15-models-pipeline-and-translation.md`.
